# Caviot Studio audit — October 2026

Historical snapshot. Since this audit the phone apps open on "Choose a template" and call the sleeve Classic Sleeve; the website and Mac app still open on the ETSYFOLGER (Foger) sleeve.

Reviewed from `main` at `2175054`, in the browser (desktop, iPhone 14, Pixel 7) and in the test suites. This lists what each control is for and the bugs that were fixed. Jason approved the six cleanups below; they are done on this branch.

## What each control is for

The default screen is the ETSYFOLGER sleeve. The orange tools change the design; Export STL writes the file.

### Project bar

- **Project name** — the name used in saved projects and downloaded files.
- **Save project** — downloads an `.icaviot` file so the design can be reopened. On a phone the same design is also kept on the device.
- **Open project** — loads an `.icaviot` file (artwork, placement, sides, settings).
- **Guide** — short instructions for measuring, the fit ring, and export.

### Top bar

- **Sleeve / Logo only / Flat plate** — which kind of model to build. Sleeve is the ETSYFOLGER (or custom / parametric) wrap. Logo only and Flat plate leave the template and build from the image.
- **OBJ** — same model as STL, as a text OBJ file. Heavier in memory.
- **Export STL** — builds the print file. Ctrl/⌘E is the shortcut. The button shows elapsed time while it runs, then the shortcut label comes back.
- **Settings** (phone) — opens the tools sheet.

### Sleeve template

- **ETSYFOLGER · your STL** — the original sleeve. Cavity and openings stay as they are; artwork is added on the outside.
- **Custom STL · upload…** — use another STL as the sleeve. Geometry outside the artwork is kept.
- **Parametric sleeve presets** — a generated sleeve from the Size & fit numbers (lighters, vapes, tubes). Hidden while ETSYFOLGER or a custom STL is selected, because those already have a shape.

### Artwork

- **Front / Back** — two sides of the sleeve, each with its own artwork.
- **Copy front to back** — duplicates the front design onto the back.
- **Link settings between sides** — size, depth and image settings follow each other.
- **Drop or click image** — PNG or JPG. Paste works on desktop. Phones can use the camera roll.
- **Rotate / mirror** (↺ ↻↻ ↻ ⇄) — turn or flip the current image before it becomes relief.
- **Text field** — lettering, up to 60 characters, as its own layer.
- **Letter spacing / Letter thickness** — only while text is the selected design. Spacing opens or closes gaps; thickness makes strokes heavier or lighter.
- **Lettering font** — 14 included open-license fonts (Bebas Neue by default), plus **Import extra fonts** and **Import font folder**.

### Place on the sleeve

- **Design width / height / rotation** — size and turn of the selected design, in millimetres and degrees. Rotation wraps past ±180 instead of stopping.
- **Around sleeve / Height from bottom** — position. Shown when you need them; width, height and rotation stay visible.
- **Uniform scale** — grows or shrinks width and height together. 100% is the size before this drag. A pinch, or editing width/height, starts a new 100%.
- **Move design on sleeve** — left-button dragging places the design. The scroll-wheel drag and one-finger phone drag work without this.
- **Even depth (logos)** — one height across the logo instead of a slope from the photo.
- **Center on front** — puts the selected design back on the front, halfway up.
- **Clear design** — removes the artwork from the sleeve.

### Depth and finish

- **Depth** — how far the selected design sticks out or is cut in, in millimetres. New designs start at 0.4 mm. On a sleeve the top is 3 mm; on a plate it is 5 mm.
- **Emboss / Deboss** — raised out of the surface, or cut into it.
- **Logo size** — scales a logo on Logo only, Flat plate, and parametric sleeves. On ETSYFOLGER and custom STL the placement width/height are what size the design, so this slider is hidden there.

### Size & fit (parametric sleeves only)

- **Device template** — starting cavity for a lighter, vape, or tube.
- **Inner width / inner depth** — the hole the object slides into.
- **Wall thickness, sleeve height, looseness** — plastic thickness, height, and extra gap.
- **Half sleeve / Whole** — 180° shell or a full 360° tube.
- **Fit ring export** — a 5 mm plain ring to test the fit before the full design.
- **Closed bottom + push-out hole** — a floor with a hole, only on a 360° sleeve.

### Flat plate and curve

- **Backing** — outline, rounded rectangle, connector bar, solid plate, or letters only. Used for text plates.
- **Backing margin** — how much plate shows around an outline backing.
- **Width / Backing thickness** — plate size.
- **Curve the plate** — bends a flat plate (diameter, amount, how much of the middle stays flat).

### Image processing

- **Remove background** — drops a colour (usually the photo’s backdrop) so it does not become relief.
- **Auto / Pick** — sample that colour from the corners, or click a pixel.
- **Removal strength** — how far from that colour still counts as background.
- **Soften edges** — feathers the cutoff.
- **Sharp edges (logos)** — contour relief instead of a soft height ramp.
- **Light parts stick out** — inverts which tones are high.
- **Mirror left ↔ right** — flips the relief.
- **Trim flat plate to image** — the plate follows the picture instead of a full rectangle. Hidden on sleeve templates.
- **Export detail** — triangle density for non-template exports. Hidden on ETSYFOLGER, which uses its own surface spacing.
- **Full-quality live preview** — the 3D view uses export resolution. Hidden on sleeve templates.
- **Smooth the surface / Smooth the outline** — blur the height or the silhouette before meshing.

### Bottom branding

- **Add bottom logo** — the DM diamond (or Design Mainline triangle) on the underside or the inside bottom. On by default. Width, height, and margins place it. This is why a sleeve with no customer artwork still exports as “emboss”: the bottom mark is raised artwork.

### Repair, layers, view

- **Make watertight** — closes holes in the current mesh and says what it changed. The sleeve cavity is kept.
- **Make template watertight** — same idea for an uploaded STL template, before artwork is applied.
- **Upload STL template…** — the only STL-template upload. The line under it says “Keeps the STL shape.” Geometry outside the artwork is kept.
- **Flatten STL into artwork** — the old path: an STL’s top view becomes a height-map image, not a template. The line under it says “Turns the STL into a picture.”
- **Undo / Redo** — the last edit to placement, depth, or image settings.
- **Front / Back / Left / Right** — snap the camera to that side.
- **Reset view** — frames the model.
- **Orbit / pan / zoom** — drag, right-drag, scroll. On a phone: one finger orbits empty space, drag on the design moves it, pinch resizes it, twist rotates it, two fingers pan.
- **Flat image** — the height map the mesh is built from, so a bad background removal is visible before export.
- **Status strip** — source, grid, triangle count, file size, dimensions, and whether the mesh is closed.
- **Print tips** — the measure → fit ring → design → slice reminder.
- **Mesh tests** — a developer self-check of the mesher. Hidden on every shape unless the page is opened with `?debug`.

### Phone and install

- **Tools sheet** — the same controls, in a bottom sheet. Done closes it. A double-tap on the design opens it.
- **Share** — on iPhone, and on Android when the browser can share a file, the finished STL opens a sheet (Share, Download, or Open) because a delayed download is blocked. Desktop Chrome and the Electron app still save with the normal download.
- **Autosave** — on a phone, the working design is stored on the device and restored after a reload. A custom STL is not stored; it has to be imported again.
- **Install / offline** — the service worker caches the app so it opens without a network after the first visit.

## Bugs found and fixed

Export was the failure Jason reported. Several paths could look like “nothing happened.”

1. **A failed sleeve export only changed the status line.** A worker crash, a throw while the height maps were built, or an empty file never toasted, and an empty buffer could still say the export succeeded. Failures now toast, and an empty file is not saved.
2. **The button looked stuck on “Forming export…”.** Building the print maps blocked the page before the label could paint, and there was no clock. The label now yields, then shows the stage and the elapsed seconds. The job still gives up only after five quiet minutes.
3. **Flat and logo exports were killed at 60 seconds.** The sleeve path already allows five minutes, which is what detailed artwork needs. The flat/logo path used a hard 60 second kill and a “Preparing…” label with no clock, so a high-detail plate looked frozen and then died. It now uses the same five-minute limit and shows elapsed time.
4. **The shortcut hint disappeared after one export.** Setting the button’s text removed the Ctrl/⌘E hint. The original label is restored when the job ends.
5. **Some filenames never saved.** A name that was only a trailing dot (`name.`), or a Windows device name such as `CON.stl`, is refused by the system and the click looks like it did nothing. Downloads now collapse extra spaces, drop a trailing dot that is not a real extension, and rename those device names (`CON` becomes `CON-file`). Ordinary names, including `Untitled design_ETSYFOLGER_emboss.stl` and `front-deboss_back-emboss.stl`, are unchanged.
6. **Desktop Safari dropped the file.** After a worker finishes, Safari ignores a scripted download the same way iOS does. Safari on a computer now gets the “file ready” sheet. Chrome, Edge, and the Electron app still use the normal download, so the desktop app’s save dialog is unchanged.
7. **Share on a phone copied the whole STL just to ask if sharing works.** That second copy can run the tab out of memory and reload it, which looks like a failed export. The check now uses a 1-byte stand-in. The real file is built only when Share is tapped.
8. **Custom template names lost their spaces.** `My Sleeve.stl` downloaded as `MySleeve`. Spaces are kept.

`exportCurrentLegacy` was unused (nothing called it) and has been removed. `buildExportMesh` stays; the text-relief browser test calls it. The CSS that hid `#advancedToggle`, `#advancedContent` and `#stats` matched nothing in the page, so that rule is gone too.

Desktop default STL bytes were checked against the same page before these edits: plain ETSYFOLGER (bottom logo on, no customer artwork), image emboss, and image deboss. The mesh bytes are built by the same worker message as before. Filename cleanup only changes names the operating system would have rejected.

## Sliders

The thumbs were 14 px on a 4 px track, which is hard to grab and under the 24 px WCAG 2.2 target (2.5.8). Material Design and the Apple HIG use a large thumb and about a 44 px touch target. Figma, Blender, and Fusion pair the slider with a number you can type, and use a modifier key for a bigger step.

Every range now has a 24 px thumb and an 8 px track with the orange fill showing the current value. The row is 36 px tall on a desktop (the track click still jumps, which is the browser’s own behaviour) and 44 px on a touch screen, where the thumb is 28 px. Hover, press, and a visible keyboard ring are there. Each slider has a number field — placement already had one, and those were kept, including rotation’s missing max so 200° can wrap to −160°. Arrow keys step once; Shift+arrow steps ten times. Double-click returns the designed default (depth 0.4 mm, uniform scale 100%, and so on), not whatever was last saved in this browser. Screen readers get the value with its unit (“0.40 millimeters”).

Dragging the number itself was left out. A scrub gesture fights the text cursor; Shift+arrow is the coarse step those other tools use.

## Cleanups Jason approved

These six were done on this branch. Logo size was left as it was: hidden on a sleeve template, still available for a flat plate, logo-only, and a parametric sleeve.

1. **Pattern is gone.** The button, the modal, the pattern drawings, and the CSS that only existed for that modal are removed. Images, paste, and lettering still add artwork. Older projects whose source label started with `pattern:` still show the name without that prefix.
2. **Add Image is gone from the layer list.** The drop zone, paste, and the phone “Add image” shortcut still add an image. The layer list keeps **T＋ Text**.
3. **One STL-template upload.** **Upload STL template…** next to the template is the only one. The top-bar button and the phone overflow item that did the same thing are gone.
4. **Mesh tests stay for developers.** The button is hidden on the sleeve, the flat plate, and the parametric sleeve. Open the page with `?debug` to show it and run the self-check.
5. **Section numbers are gone.** “Place on the sleeve” and “Size & fit” no longer both say 03. The phone layout already stripped those prefixes; the desktop labels now match.
6. **The two STL buttons say what they do.** Under Upload STL template…: “Keeps the STL shape.” Under Flatten STL into artwork: “Turns the STL into a picture.”

## What was exercised

Desktop Chrome, iPhone 14 and Pixel 7, against the app served from `dist/`.

- Plain ETSYFOLGER (bottom logo on), image emboss and image deboss were exported from `main` (`2175054`) and from this branch. All three STL files match byte for byte, including the download names.
- A photo plus the word CAVIOT landed on layers. Copy between sides, undo, the guide, Save project, and a font file all worked.
- A small closed STL imported as a custom template (`Template ready`) and exported a valid STL named `Untitled design_tiny-sleeve_plain.stl`.
- Flat plate at export detail 400 produced a valid STL (2,728 triangles).
- Filenames: `CON.stl` and `AUX.obj` gain a `-file` suffix, `name.` loses the stray dot, and the normal sleeve names are unchanged.
- On iPhone 14 the tools sheet opens, pinch still resizes a design, and twist still rotates it. The same sheet opens on a Pixel 7.

## Left as they are

- **Closed bottom with a push-out hole** can report reversed winding (duplicate directed edges). Fixing the winding would change image-export bytes, so it was not touched.
- **Desktop export names follow the selected design.** Phones name every side (`front-deboss_back-emboss`). That split is intentional.
- **Bottom logo on** makes a sleeve with no customer image export as emboss, not plain. The mark is real raised geometry.
